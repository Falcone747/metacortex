From correlation to causation

Pearl's Ladder of Causation — three rungs that distinguish what dashboards, A/B tests, and counterfactuals can each answer.

L1

Association — P(Y|X)

What dashboards do. Seeing. Given X, how does Y change? Sufficient for prediction; insufficient for decision because every confounder corrupts the reading.

L2

Intervention — P(Y|do(X))

What experiments do. Doing. If we set X, what does Y become? Cuts the back-door confounding paths. The minimum bar for any real business decision.

L3

Counterfactual — P(Y_x | X', Y')

What retrospection does. Imagining. Given what happened, what would have happened if X had been different? The unique capability of structural causal models. The basis for genuine organisational learning.

Sample SCM — Manufacturing Delays

A real diagnostic from a B2B manufacturer with 80 employees. The metacortex surfaces a counter-intuitive leverage point: the delay isn't caused by production capacity — it's caused by priority attribution.

Finding

Simulating do(priority_quality = 0.8) vs status quo yields a 23% reduction in delivery delays with the same capacity. No headcount change.

Method

1,000 Monte Carlo samples per intervention, twin-network counterfactuals, double-robust estimation. All reproducible in the app.

The architecture

Six layers. Each one observable, testable, replaceable. Open source, deployable on any stack.

L6InterfaceQuestion-answering for decision-makers, calibrated uncertainty, red-teaming.
L5Bayesian loopContinuous update of SCMs from observed outcomes, doubly-robust estimation, drift detection.
L4Counterfactual engine — "what if we did X?" with full uncertainty propagation.
L3SCMsStructural causal models per sub-system (acquisition, operations, RH, finance).
L2IngestionMulti-modal: transactions, HR, market signals, weak signals. Event-driven.
L1DataWarehouse + lineage + quality. Auditable, replayable, versioned.

Why now

The classical business intelligence stack answers L1. Most experimentation stacks reach L2 on isolated A/B tests. Almost no enterprise stack reaches L3 in production — and L3 is where the most valuable decisions live (irreversible ones).

The trap

Most enterprise AI today is L1 dressed up as L2/L3. Predictions dressed as recommendations. Recommendations dressed as decisions. Every "AI strategy" that doesn't explicitly distinguish these three levels is conflating them — and the organisation learns to mistrust data.

The leverage

Crossing from L1 to L3 in production is not a tool problem — it's a methodological discipline problem. The tool is small. The discipline is large. metacortex makes the discipline executable: SCMs as code, interventions as reproducible experiments, counterfactuals as deployable artifacts.
