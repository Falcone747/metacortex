From correlation to cause.

Pearl's Ladder of Causation — three rungs that distinguish what dashboards, A/B tests, and counterfactuals can each answer.

L1 · Association

P(Y | X)

What dashboards do. Seeing. Given X, how does Y change? Sufficient for prediction; insufficient for decision because every confounder corrupts the reading.

L2 · Intervention

P(Y | do(X))

What experiments do. Doing. If we set X, what does Y become? Cuts back-door confounding paths. The minimum bar for any real business decision.

L3 · Counterfactual

P(Yx | X', Y')

What retrospection does. Imagining. Given what happened, what would have happened? The unique capability of structural causal models. The basis for genuine organisational learning.

The diagnostic before the model.

No SCM without first mapping the system. Three parallel cartographies before any code.

Step 1

Causal Loop Diagram

Actors, flows, feedback loops (R vs B), stocks, delays. The first map. Often reveals that what people call "the problem" is downstream of two loops they don't see.

Step 2

Decision map

Not the org chart. Who actually decides what, with what information, under what time constraint. In 80% of cases the org chart lies about who decides.

Step 3

Mental models

Beliefs, dominant metaphors (machine / organism / ecosystem), contradictions across departments that don't know they oppose each other.

The trap

Most enterprise AI today is L1 dressed up as L2/L3. Predictions dressed as recommendations. Recommendations dressed as decisions. Every "AI strategy" that doesn't explicitly distinguish these three levels is conflating them — and the organisation learns to mistrust data.

The leverage

Crossing from L1 to L3 in production is not a tool problem — it's a methodological discipline problem. The tool is small. The discipline is large. metacortex makes the discipline executable: SCMs as code, interventions as reproducible experiments, counterfactuals as deployable artifacts.
